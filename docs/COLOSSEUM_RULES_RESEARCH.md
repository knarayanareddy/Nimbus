# Colosseum’s current 2026 hackathon: eligibility and submission research

**Research snapshot:** 12 October 2026 (CEST). Public pages and rules only; no registration or credential interaction. The live public Arena route `https://colosseum.com/arena/hackathon` resolves to an account-creation/sign-in flow, so I did not enter it.

## Bottom line

The current event is **Crypto World’s Fair** (also styled “Crypto World’s Fair Hackathon”), Colosseum’s **Fall 2026** event—not the spring **Solana Frontier** hackathon. The event’s official rules set the competition period to **September 14, 2026, 6:00 a.m. PT through October 12, 2026, 11:59 p.m. PT**. The official public event page gives the submission due date as October 12; the legal rules give the exact cutoff and PT timezone. The event page describes a public-blockchain, multi-ecosystem contest and enumerates the eight ecosystem tracks relevant to the question: Solana, Ethereum, Hyperliquid, Base, Tempo, Arbitrum, Zcash, and Robinhood Chain.

For a pre-existing product such as Nimbus, the safest source-grounded reading is: **pre-hackathon development is not categorically barred by Colosseum’s current general eligibility FAQ, but only work done during the contest period is judged and relevant past development must be disclosed.** Neither the current official FAQ nor the current official rules retrieved here say a project must never have been submitted to another hackathon. That is not an affirmative finding that Nimbus is eligible: the rules separately require compliance with eligibility restrictions, the product must fit the rules’ concept of the competition, and Colosseum retains disqualification discretion. If Nimbus itself (or an earlier materially identical version) was submitted previously, ask Colosseum to confirm how that history should be disclosed and whether it changes eligibility. Do not represent the 2026 event as Solana-only or apply Frontier’s expired dates/rules to it.

## Event identity, timing, timezone

Colosseum’s hackathon index labels the live event “Crypto World’s Fair,” “Fall 2026,” and “Live Now”; it labels Frontier separately as “Spring 2026.” [1] The event page says “Submissions due October 12, 2026.” [2] The **current event’s official legal rules** provide the controlling exact timing: “The Contest Period starts at 6:00am PT on September 14, 2026 and ends at 11:59pm PT on October 12, 2026.” They also state: “The winners will be announced by December 5, 2026.” [3]

The current event landing page URL is `https://colosseum.com/worldsfair`; Colosseum’s general event FAQ also confirms Crypto World’s Fair as the current Fall 2026 campaign. [1][4] “PT” is the rule’s exact timezone notation; do not substitute an unconfirmed local deadline or infer that the landing-page date alone means midnight in the entrant’s timezone. The official legal rules say Colosseum’s computer is the official timekeeping device and entrants are responsible for local conversion. [3]

## Official current rules and eligibility

The current rules are linked directly from the public official rules PDF at [3]. They describe this as an innovation challenge using “public blockchains,” with design/UX, development, business, and crypto-platform competence. [3]

Relevant exact current-rule requirements:

- **Age and geographic/legal limits:** entrants must be at least 18 or the age of majority in their country, whichever is older, with specified sanctions/export-control exclusions and “Void where prohibited.” [3]
- **Per-person/team limits:** “Entrant may only be a Member of one (1) Team. A Team may only submit one (1) Project Submission at a time.” [3]
- **Registration/submission:** every member must register by **11:59 p.m. PT October 12, 2026**; the team leader uploads the project submission before the end of the entry period. [3]
- **No blanket rule requiring work to begin on September 14:** the current official general FAQ says, verbatim, “Teams may begin development before the hackathon, but products are judged only on the work completed between the competition’s start and end dates.” It also says, “Builders may use pre-existing code, but teams must disclose all relevant past development work in the submission form.” [4]
- The same FAQ frames eligibility as building “a new product that hasn’t raised significant funding” and says the event is not intended for established companies that have built the same product for years and already raised venture funding. [4]
- The FAQ warns that misrepresenting product development history or failing to disclose relevant information can lead to disqualification, a future-event ban, or prize revocation. Its footnote clarifies that “Pre-existing code” does not mean others’ open-source code; composing with existing crypto protocols is encouraged. [4]

**Prior-project / never-submitted question:** I found no “must be new,” “never submitted,” or equivalent prior-hackathon exclusion in the retrieved current Crypto World’s Fair rules or Colosseum FAQ. The FAQ explicitly permits pre-start development and pre-existing code subject to disclosure and judging only in-period progress. Thus a project that existed before the event and was not previously submitted is **not automatically disqualified by the published language found**. However, the rules do not expressly answer whether a prior submission to another contest, earlier Colosseum event, or a substantially similar incarnation imposes additional conditions. A previous submission is not a reason to conceal the history: disclose it in the submission and ask Colosseum if unsure.

## Multi-chain scope and judging

The official event page calls this “An online hackathon bringing together builders and founders across all crypto ecosystems” and says it is “an open competition” for technologies and communities shaping the blockchain ecosystem. [2] It states that track prizes are awarded in addition to the overall awards, and lists these eight ecosystems: **Solana, Ethereum, Hyperliquid, Base, Tempo, Arbitrum, Zcash, Robinhood Chain**. [2] This confirms the listed chain ecosystems are in the event scope; it does not establish that every listed ecosystem has identical track rules, tooling, prize amounts, or operational support.

The current rules say each submission is judged by Colosseum and a panel chosen by the administrator against these criteria (quotes paraphrased here, exact categories): **Functionality; Potential Impact; Novelty; UX; Open-source; Business Plan.** In the current contest, the impact/UX/open-source criteria refer to the broader **crypto ecosystem** and blockchain primitives, not specifically Solana. [3] The event page separately says the Colosseum team reviews submissions and determines overall winners, while track judges provide feedback/evaluation in dedicated tracks. [2] General Colosseum FAQ describes product/execution, market size, founder communication, viability, and traction as broader assessment dimensions; treat it as explanatory FAQ, not a replacement for the event-specific rule criteria. [4]

## Submission requirements and public resources

The official rules require each team member to register, with one submission per team and the team leader responsible for uploading it before the deadline. [3] Colosseum’s general FAQ says the portal is available from the platform dashboard after joining; every team member needs an account and the team leader adds them during submission. It lists expected submission material including product description, chain/tools integrated, team backgrounds, location, logo/graphic, GitHub repository (private allowed if review access is granted), a 2–3 minute presentation video, a demo video of up to 3 minutes, and go-to-market/demand-validation information. [4] Because those details are FAQ guidance rather than directly confirmed in the current rules PDF, verify the live form/requirements if the account is available; do not assume the account-gated portal’s exact fields from a prior event.

## Distinguishing earlier 2026 event materials

The official Frontier campaign page and its **Solana Frontier Hackathon Official Rules** are for the **April 6–May 11, 2026** Spring event, with an April 6 6:00 a.m. PT start and May 11 11:59 p.m. PT end in its rules. Frontier’s criteria include impact on the Solana ecosystem; it is not the current Crypto World’s Fair event. [6][7] The January 2026 Colosseum Codex schedule announcement also confirms the fall edition dates September 28–November 2, but this is superseded for the current event’s actual dates by the event-specific Crypto World’s Fair legal rules: September 14–October 12. [8] A calendar/newsletter schedule and a current event’s legal terms need not coincide; for eligibility and deadlines, prioritize the current event’s own legal rules and landing page.

## Open questions / cautions

1. **No express previously-submitted project clause located:** there is no clear current-event rule stating whether a prior external or earlier Colosseum submission is disqualifying, acceptable with disclosure, or subject to a material-change test. Obtain written Colosseum confirmation for Nimbus if its earlier submission history matters.
2. **Current legal rules govern; FAQ is broader:** the general FAQ allows prior development subject to disclosure, but no separate FAQ sentence specifically says “previously submitted projects are eligible.” Do not turn silence into a guarantee.
3. **Date already reached on snapshot day:** the official deadline is October 12 at 11:59 p.m. PT; public signup route required account interaction, which was intentionally not attempted. I did not inspect any authenticated submission portal or establish whether it remained technically open at the exact time of research.
4. **Track-specific technical/prize detail:** the event page identifies ecosystem tracks and awards, but the retrieved public sources do not resolve every track’s technical acceptance criteria or whether a Solana-first project using USDC/rainfall oracle integrations may claim a specific non-Solana track. Choose the applicable track based on where the submitted product actually operates; ask Colosseum/track organizers for confirmation.
5. **Full legal restrictions matter:** review the current rules PDF for jurisdiction, sanctions, consent, intellectual-property, and other conditions; this summary is not legal advice.
